# Full-size crutch foot repair prototype

This is an unfinished prototype. No full-score demonstration or submission claim is made.

The instruction is “Prepare this crutch for its owner.” The rubber foot is worn through; the aluminum tube projects 4 mm beyond its remaining sole. Worn-through ferrules can expose the metal tube and slip under a person, as described by the Nottingham and Nottinghamshire NHS falls-prevention guidance. The instruction does not identify the hazard or prescribe replacement.

The original 22 mm shaft ended inside that solid tip at z=30 mm; a same-diameter tube continuation reaches the reconstructed physical seating stop at z=8 mm. This 22 mm continuation is explicitly reconstructed, not a robot grip feature. Its visible 2 mm wall is an estimate. ## Contact model

The new foot has an 8 mm sole, 38 mm lower outside diameter, 30 mm upper outside diameter, and 45 mm height. Twelve radial compliant elements form its bore. Each element has a 20,000 N/m spring, 3.0 N s/m damping, 1.5 g lumped mass, and 0.5 mm inward / 1.5 mm outward travel bounds. Their free contact planes are at radius 10.8 mm around the 11 mm shaft. Static/dynamic contact friction is 0.8/0.65. Nominal centered insertion therefore compresses each element about 0.2 mm and supplies roughly 48 N total normal reaction. These are disclosed approximate rubber properties, not measured manufacturer force data. These stiffness and damping values are estimates; they are not a measured rubber constitutive model. Each angular element spans the bore, so axial variation in local rubber compression is simplified.

The bore has a 5 mm entry chamfer opening to radius 11.7 mm at its mouth. The chamfer converts insertion contact into radial rubber compression. The shaft bottoms against the actual sole collider. The worn foot has lost its bottom 12 mm and has a relaxed bore radius of 10.98 mm; its remaining rubber still grips weakly. No joint connects either foot to the crutch. No attachment flag, snap, proximity trigger, seating force activation or scripted object motion is used.

Each bore wall and entry bevel share one convex collider. A scale-preserving conditioning transform authors convex vertices 1000 times larger under a 0.001 transform, and native cooked-boundary queries verify the intended inner planes. The convex subset clips 12.07 mm³ per sector from the former outer bevel protrusion, preserving the full inner contact profile and all explicit physical parameters. This is not exact-union equivalence. Outer shell hulls use the same conditioning transform. The outer shell is treated as rigid and the bore as radial elements; continuous circumferential rubber deformation, rubber hysteresis and tread deformation are approximated. The crutch's long bent-shaft convex hull is replaced by analytic cylinders/capsules following its original centerline, so the collider does not fill empty space beside the shaft. The lower cylinder is represented by two collinear cylinders at native z=8–68 mm and 68–500 mm, radius 11 mm, on the same rigid body. Their closed union equals the prior 492 mm cylinder; the seam is 23 mm above the 45 mm ferrule mouth. Native mass and center-of-mass position are bit-identical; inertia differs by at most 3.7e-11 kg m² after native generation. This subdivision addresses a spurious mouth contact from the long analytic cylinder without changing shaft size, mass, contact properties or available grasp surfaces.

The full crutch lies diagonally on the unmodified bench. It is not clamped or held by an invisible fixture. A real operator could reproduce the task with a full-size 22 mm crutch, matching dry rubber foot, old through-worn foot and ordinary bench.

The twelve full-depth sectors move radially with uniform displacement along their bore walls. This is an explicit coarse approximation of axial coupling; local axial strain is omitted. Independent axial sections would create inward wall-top ledges when adjacent sections expand by different amounts. Each full-depth sector preserves the aggregate mass, center of mass, inertia, stiffness, damping, travel, force cap and friction of the earlier segmented model. The entry bevel shares its inner lower endpoint with the vertical bore wall at z=40 mm, and the single convex collider eliminates the former internal wall-top contact face at that boundary.

## Validation status

The current model has completed contact and retention component controls: matching foot retained under its own weight during unsupported tilt; the released preassembled assembly scored 1.0 and the otherwise identical held assembly 0.5; an oversized 24 mm bore foot separated and scored 0. A loose 50 g foot resting on the ordinary bench or native forearm produces approximately 0.4905 N reaction and is correctly excluded from unsupported retention. These controls do not demonstrate removal and replacement. No 8 N pull or patient-load result is claimed for this model. Two complete full-score repairs remain required.
