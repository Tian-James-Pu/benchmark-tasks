Recover a physically obstructed Jetson Nano controller cooling fan. Two fresh scripted trials with the same mechanics and scorer each reached 1.0 in 292 control steps (293 recorded frames). Idle and forceful-press controls scored 0.0; a held-strip control and an isolated hands-near-tray control scored 0.3. Those trials preceded a visual-only correction moving the power switch’s white I mark to its depressed ON end. The generated switch scene is identical after removing that non-colliding mark; mechanics, collisions and scoring are unchanged. The corrected-scene demo also reached 1.0 in 292 steps; its media is the submitted demonstration. The corrected-label Dream-1 run completed 600 steps at 0.0; its genuine model video is submitted alongside the demonstration.

The instruction is: “Restore the controller cooling fan and put the removed debris in the parts tray.” The controller is secured to an insulating service plate. A loose nylon cable-tie offcut passes through the wire guard and physically obstructs the impeller. The robot can draw out its exposed end without reaching through the guard; a physical inline power rocker is also available. The objective does not prescribe an isolation sequence for an external guarded pull.

The custom insulating service plate is 115 × 95 × 3 mm, centred 15 mm toward negative Y from the board datum. Four nylon supports are 6 mm diameter × 9 mm long, at board-relative (x, y) positions (−44, −39), (42, −39), (−44, 19), (42, 19) mm. Visible fixed screw heads are 5.6 × 1.5 mm on the board, 5.0 × 1.2 mm at the bench plate and 5.8 × 2.0 mm at the guard. The four insulated fan leads are 0.9 mm diameter; their source-coordinate routing and plug dimensions are in `service_hardware.py`. The blue tray has a 3 mm floor and walls and a 100 mm interior.

The board is the manufacturer’s [Jetson Nano 2GB developer-kit STEP](https://developer.nvidia.com/jetson-nano-2gb-developer-kit-3d-cad-step-model). The fan frame and separate impeller come from [Delta’s AFB0405MA-A STEP](https://www.delta-fan.com/Download/3D/AFB0405MA-A.stp). The [NVIDIA user guide](https://developer.nvidia.com/embedded/learn/jetson-nano-2gb-devkit-user-guide) specifies a 5 V fan header and lists AFB0405MA-AFGE as compatible. The [AFGE specification](https://media.digikey.com/pdf/Data%20Sheets/Delta%20PDFs/AFB0405MA-AFGE_Ds.pdf) gives 40 × 40 × 10 mm, 14 g, 5 V and 5,000 RPM. The mechanical CAD is the base A variant; the AFGE PWM/tach wiring is represented separately. J7 routing follows CAD solid 350 and NVIDIA’s carrier drawing. The AFGE sheet specifies clockwise rotation viewed from the nameplate side. In the oriented CAD, the frame struts are below the impeller (strut annulus z=0–2.7 mm; rotor z=3.7–9.7 mm). Positive Z rotation is clockwise when viewed from that underside and is the forward direction scored here.

The offcut section follows the [Panduit SG100M-C manufacturer bulletin](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0213/7895/7412/files/110209838.pdf?v=1718861801): PA66, 3.0 mm wide and 1.0 mm thick. An 80.2 mm offcut is used; the model omits the small serrations and tapered end.

The [Orion G40-2 guard STEP](https://orionfans.com/3d/G40-2.stp) and [drawing](https://orionfans.com/productFiles/datasheet/G40.pdf) provide the 32 mm square mount pattern, 1.6 mm wire diameter and 4.25 mm spacing between rings. The supply is based on the [5 V, 3 A switched USB-C adapter](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5802) and its [DC-cord drawing](https://cdn-shop.adafruit.com/product-files/5802/XSD-0503000SUSD.pdf). The drawing specifies a 90 ± 5 mm connector-to-switch lead and 3.8 mm cable diameter. The 304 rocker housing and its detent are simplified.

Prepare source meshes in a separate Python environment with `cadquery==2.8.0` and `numpy`, then run `python build_assets.py` and `python build_service_assets.py` from this folder. These download STEP files to `~/.cache/jetson-fan-recovery` and record source URLs and hashes. The expected source and derived-cache hashes are listed in `asset_manifest.json`. Both builders verify the pinned STEP hashes, including cached inputs, and fail explicitly on a mismatch. Manufacturer CAD is downloaded, not redistributed in the task folder. The two service tools use the NVIDIA SimReady loader at its pinned dataset revision and are downloaded on first load.

There is one fixed layout, no randomization, and no stochastic demo seed. The last control maintained approximately 4,999 RPM for 15.4 seconds and satisfied containment; only the required hand clearance prevented full score. Neither forceful manipulation nor the intrusion proxies demonstrate fracture detection.

The scorer returns 0 until the offcut’s centreline clears a 29 mm radius around the fan centre, 0.3 for clearance alone, 0.6 for full containment in the tray, and 1.0 when contained with the fan continuously above 4,000 RPM for 10 seconds. Every tier also requires board, fan and guard origins within 2 mm of their mounting positions and rotation within 3 degrees. Entering the powered rotor volume, or entering while the rotor coasts above 1 rad/s, with a sampled finger surface permanently invalidates the trial. Source collision-mesh oriented bounds for either tool are also checked against the 37 × 37 × 7.4 mm blade envelope, centred 6.7 mm above the fan base. This is a conservative access boundary, not fracture detection. After isolation, coasting protection remains active until speed first reaches at most 1 rad/s; subsequent deliberate manual rotation is allowed. Containment and full credit require at least 20 mm between the debris and each oriented finger box reconstructed from actual gripper pose and symmetric joint-7 kinematics and service-tool world bounding box; holding the debris in the tray earns clearance credit only. Rotation must be in the normal forward direction. Containment checks every offcut corner against the tray’s 98 mm square interior, height 2–20.5 mm and speed below 20 mm/s. Score reads state; it does not move any object or advance the operation history. In a physical trial, compare before/after photographs and inspect the assembly: any new visible crack, broken blade, detached wire or missing fastener invalidates every tier. The rigid simulation uses only the stated pose and intrusion proxies; it does not simulate those failures or certify that real damage is absent.

The scene uses the original-scale CAD meshes with 0.08 mm fan and 0.15 mm board tessellation tolerances. The rotor uses an SDF collider at resolution 256 (approximately 0.143 mm grid spacing). The guard uses source triangle colliders. Small electronic packages are visible only; principal board housings and connectors collide. The free offcut has a scaled-coordinate convex collider, 50 micrometre contact offset, 3 × 1 mm section, 80.2 mm length, PA66 density 1,140 kg/m³ and mass 0.274 g. Its resting obstruction arises from gravity and contact; no latch, attachment, contact-state rule or score rule holds or releases it.

Important approximations: the offcut is rigid; bending and fracture are not simulated. Rotor mass is estimated at 2.7 g, with its inertia and centre of mass calculated from the manufacturer CAD. Motor torque, velocity gain and passive coast-down drag are estimates, not manufacturer dynamometer measurements. Electrical and thermal behavior are omitted. The power rocker changes motor drive from its actual joint angle; it never changes object pose. The fan/board/guard are fastened components; structural fracture is outside the model. Wiring is fixed, strain-relieved visual geometry outside the extraction path. The service tools are single rigid bodies and their internal joints are not used. These limitations must be retained in the final fidelity description.

The completed mechanism controls showed approximately 4,999 RPM unobstructed and an essentially fixed rotor angle with the offcut present, both without and with the source wire guard. These were feasibility diagnostics, not full task demonstrations.
